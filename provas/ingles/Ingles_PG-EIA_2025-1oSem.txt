Part 1 - Read the article “How ‘green’ electricity from wood harms the planet — and
people” and answer the questions 1 to 6 according to the text.
Question 1. How does the article exemplify environmental racism in relation to wood-pellet
plants and local communities?
A) Enviva’s expansion into international markets that target underdeveloped regions.
B) The establishment of renewable-energy subsidies that prioritize profit over local well-
being, which restrain the political power of communities.
C) The concentration of wood-pellet plants near predominantly Black, marginalized
communities, leading to disproportionate exposure to harmful pollutants and increased long-
term health risks.
D) Job creation in economically disadvantaged areas, which diverts attention from
environmental concerns.
Question 2. What major announcement did Enviva Biomass make in September 2014?
A) They would close their existing facilities.
B) They were halting all wood production.
C) They were moving their headquarters to Europe.
D) They planned to open a major facility in Hamlet, NC.
Question 3. Why did John Sterman’s research suggest that wood bioenergy could
exacerbate climate change until 2100?
A) Trees take decades to regrow and absorb the carbon dioxide released from burning wood,

B) Wood burning is more efficient than coal but still emits harmful pollutants.
C) Wood pellets are made from high-quality lumber, which increases deforestation rates.
D) The emissions from wood bioenergy are fully absorbed by the atmosphere within a few
years.
Question 4. What health issues have residents near the Enviva plant reported?
A) Skin rashes and asthma.
B) Breathing problems and asthma.
C) Eye irritation and breathing problems.
D) Increased allergy symptoms.

B) They negotiated with Spectrum, realizing that a settlement might result in better
environmental protections.
C) They successfully blocked the construction of the plant.
D) They received full government support to oppose the plant due to health concerns.
Question 6. What was the outcome of the legal actions against Enviva in North Carolina?
A) The plant was shut down.
B) No changes were made to their operations.
C) They won a lawsuit against environmental groups.
D) They had to invest in pollution-capture devices.
